# Status - 64 Frosted glass chrome

**2026-11-17:** Implemented on web and Flutter in one PR. Web unit tests, lint and build pass; Flutter `design_system` tests pass with three goldens regenerated (glass surfaces light/dark, navigation). Contrast was checked numerically (see ADR-074). Not measured: real-device FPS, Safari, low-end Android.

**2026-11-25:** Web dashboard reworked to one shared background with no live blur (ADR-078, which supersedes ADR-074 for web; mobile unchanged). Checked in the browser as super admin on the Agencies and Licenses pages: light and dark, grid scrolled with 60 browser-only preview rows, filter menu, drawer, modules dialog, dropdown, account menu, phone menu at 375px and the licence-required screen. Dashboard, shared UI, design tokens and sign-in unit tests pass; type check and production build pass. Dark secondary text is about 4.5:1 or better at the strongest point of each glow (computed from the token colours). Not checked: sign-in screens (they redirect while signed in), toasts, command palette, high-contrast theme, tenant (non super admin) screens, and frame times.

**2026-11-25 (readability pass):** less grain, darker secondary text and borders, separate placeholder colour (see ADR-078). Checked on Agencies and Licenses in light, and on Agencies in dark and at 375 px. Contrast was calculated on pessimistic backgrounds: secondary text 8.4:1 light and 6.4:1 dark; control borders 3.1:1; placeholder 4.4:1 light and 4.7:1 dark (higher on the real field background).

**2026-11-25 (details and calendar):** read-only details show the label beside its value through one global style (`.detail-item` and its older names, also used by `lpg-detail-item`); per-screen copies were removed. The calendar pop-up keeps a standard 20rem width instead of the width of its input, and its month arrows are square again. Checked in the browser: agency details (desktop and 375 px), license details, flag details and the calendar (dark and light, with a browser-only sample flag). Screens that need an agency sign-in were checked by tests, lint and a template structure scan only.

**2026-11-25 (danger buttons):** solid danger buttons use one dark red (`--color-action-danger`, `#b91c1c`) with white text in every theme (6.5:1; the lighter red used before in dark was 3.0:1). `--color-status-danger` is unchanged for red text, icons, and outlined and text danger buttons. Checked in the browser: Close agency (dark, plus hover), Revoke license (dark) and a browser-only confirmation popup (dark and light). High contrast was not viewed.

**2026-11-25 (drawer scrolling):** no live blur or filter was found on the drawer. Its scroll areas are see-through, and Chrome was redrawing their content during scrolling; they now use `will-change: scroll-position` (they get greyscale instead of subpixel text smoothing). Measured on a test page in headless Chromium using the real tokens and background rules, not the signed-in app: about 540 raster tasks per scroll run before, 2 after. A flat opaque drawer gave about 540 too, so the layered background was not the cause. No variant dropped frames on that run, so the reported lag was not reproduced. Dialogs and the main page scroll were not changed or measured.

**2026-11-25 (tooltips and drawer dividers):**

- `lpg-page-header` and `lpg-empty-state` no longer leave their `title` input on the element as an HTML attribute (the browser showed it as a second tooltip). The other native `title` tooltips became `pTooltip`; iframe titles stay, for screen readers.
- New `lpg-info-tip` (shared UI) is the one info button: tap or click keeps it open; a second press, a press elsewhere, Escape or a scroll closes it. PrimeNG's own tap and click handling hid the tooltip at once. Used by the page header, linked devices, tenant configuration, the permissions dialog and notification settings. Use it for any new info icon.
- Drawer dividers run edge to edge (to the scrollbar where one shows): `--drawer-bleed` in `styles.css`.
- Checked: unit tests, lint and build; the signed-in License Issuance header and License Details drawer; real taps and mouse clicks in headless Chromium on the local app with every API call faked. Not viewed: the other info buttons and the invoice, customer and audit drawers (they need an agency sign-in), Safari or a physical phone.

**2026-11-25 (closed drawers and card edges):**

- Drawer and dialog content stays alive after it closes. `lpg-form-field` now stops showing the earlier submit's errors once its form is reset; the next submit shows them again. The upload box and the compliance documents panel clear themselves when their drawer closes, and the order customer search and delivery photo picker clear when their form resets. New components with state outside a form need the same care.
- Cards have one border and no shadow. The "double border" was `--component-card-shadow`, a 1px highlight along the top edge; the token is removed. The shadows on `.card`, the dispatch board and the onboarding wizard card are removed too. Dialogs, drawers, menus, toasts and tooltips keep their shadows.
- Checked: unit tests and lint for all projects (not e2e), dashboard type check, build and Prettier. Drawer reset: the Issue a license drawer on the local app (closed with Cancel and with X). Cards: the agency Home page in headless Chromium with every API call faked, dark and light (17 cards, no shadow, no nested border at the edges). Not viewed: other drawers, the dispatch board, the onboarding wizard, settings pages, high contrast.

**2026-11-25 (sidebar selection):** the sliding highlight and its measuring script are gone. Each nav item fades its own fill, together with its text colour and weight, over `--motion-duration-medium`. The text had changed at once while the fill was still travelling, which read as flicker. Inter is now requested as the variable font (`wght@400..700`) so weight can fade. Checked frame by frame in headless Chromium (dark theme, open sidebar and rail). Not viewed: light, high contrast, phone menu. Fonts still come from Google Fonts, so an offline install falls back to a system font.

**2026-11-26 (drawer edge looked 2px):** the edge was drawn at 34% white in dark mode (`--surface-acrylic-border`, a more specific rule than the faint `--glass-border` meant for drawers). Both drawer edges now use `--glass-border`, like the sidebar. The colour fix and the width change in the next entry both apply. An earlier reading of a second, partly lit pixel at 1.25x and 1.5x came from Playwright's emulated scale factor, not from real screen scaling.

**2026-11-26 (sidebar highlight and drawer edge):**

- The sidebar highlight slides again, quickly (`--motion-duration-small`, standard easing), replacing the per-item fade from the 2026-11-25 entry above, which was too slow and looked odd. The fill is solid brand blue (white text) in dark and light, so text and fill must never be out of step: a timed colour change cannot do that on a long jump. The sliding fill now clips an inert copy of the nav list drawn in the selected colours. Text under the fill is always the selected colour and text off it never is, on every frame, for any distance, rapid clicks, group changes, the rail and the phone menu. Real links show only the unselected colours; the fill also carries the focus ring when the selected item has focus. Font weight is no longer animated, so `index.html` is back to the static Inter weights. Anything added to the nav list's markup must also go in the copy (a unit test checks the two match).
- Drawer edge: there is only one border (no second line from a wrapper, pseudo-element, theme rule or shadow). It is 1 CSS px, which is two device pixels on a 2x screen. `--hairline-scale` (tokens.css) now makes the drawer edge exactly one device pixel on 2x and 4x screens; high contrast keeps its thicker 2px edge. Playwright's `deviceScaleFactor` emulation smears a 1px border at 1.25x and 1.5x, a test artefact: a real forced scale factor gives one crisp pixel there already.
- Checked in headless Chromium with every API call and WebSocket faked: drawer edge pixels at real scale factors 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3, dark and light, right-side drawer (the app has no left-side drawers; the left rule was checked on a synthetic element); sidebar frames at 5% speed, dark and light, a long jump across groups; fill and text geometry for the open sidebar, rail, phone menu, high contrast and reduced motion; rapid clicks. Not checked: Safari and Firefox, a real Windows or Retina display, a tenant with badges on nav items, real-speed feel on a slow machine.
